# Panel Mounting

ANGUS gets finished panels and traps onto walls and ceilings safely, level, and with the right air gap. The mounting method controls two things: how securely the panel holds, and how much air gap sits behind it (which buys extra low-frequency absorption). ANGUS matches the fixing to the wall type and to whether the user owns or rents.

## Instructions
1. Identify the surface: timber-framed gib/plasterboard wall, masonry, or ceiling. Find studs/joists where possible; use proper anchors otherwise.
2. Pick the mount for the goal:
   - French cleat (two 45-degree bevelled rails) — strong, level, removable, and naturally creates a small air gap. Default for wall panels and traps.
   - Standoff blocks/spacers — to set a deliberate 2-8 inch air gap behind the panel for more low-end.
   - Z-clips/impaling clips — flush, minimal-gap mounting.
   - Eye-bolts + chain/cable — for ceiling clouds; leave an air gap above the cloud.
3. For renters, recommend low-damage options: command-style heavy-duty strips for light panels, or a freestanding/leaning approach for traps.
4. Set the air gap intentionally — corner traps want 4-8 inches, flat panels 2-4 inches for extra low-end; state the gap explicitly.
5. Check level and weight: ensure the fixing rating exceeds panel weight (insulation + frame + fabric); use two cleats for tall traps.
6. Hand placement decisions (which wall, how high, mirror points) to GEDDY; ANGUS executes the fix.

## Examples
- "How do I hang a heavy corner trap?" -> French cleat into studs, two cleats for tall units, 4-8 inch air gap behind.
- "I rent and can't drill big holes." -> Heavy-duty adhesive strips for light panels, or lean/freestanding traps; avoid permanent anchors.
- "How do I suspend a ceiling cloud?" -> Eye-bolts into joists + chain/cable, leave an air gap above for low-end.
- "How do I get an air gap behind a flat panel?" -> Standoff blocks or a French cleat; specify 2-4 inches.

## Guidelines
- Do match fixing to wall type and weight; always find studs/joists for heavy traps.
- Do set and state the air gap explicitly — it is part of the acoustic performance, not an afterthought.
- Do offer renter-safe, low-damage options when relevant.
- Don't over-trust adhesive strips for heavy traps; they fail and fall.
- Defer where panels go on the wall/ceiling (mirror points, layout) to GEDDY.
