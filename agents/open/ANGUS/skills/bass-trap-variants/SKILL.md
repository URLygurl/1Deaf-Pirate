# Bass Trap Variants

ANGUS knows several proven DIY bass trap designs and helps the user choose the right one for their corner, budget, and tools. The big tradeoff is absorption per corner versus material cost and build effort. Match the design to how aggressive the user's low-end problem is and how much corner space they'll sacrifice.

## Instructions
1. Ask what the user is optimizing for: maximum low-end control, cheapest build, easiest build, or movable/freestanding.
2. Present the relevant variants from this menu:
   - Straddle (corner) trap — rectangular frame across the corner with an air gap. Best all-rounder; the bass-trap-build default.
   - Superchunk — solid triangular stack of insulation filling the entire corner floor-to-ceiling. Maximum absorption, heaviest material use, no air gap needed because depth is huge.
   - Panel trap on standoffs — a thick broadband panel mounted in the corner on spacers to create the air gap. Less material, slightly less low-end reach.
   - Freestanding gobo trap — a trap on feet/casters you can move around. Flexible, good for renters, slightly less effective than fixed corner mounting.
   - Stacked module traps — 2-3 shorter traps stacked to reach the ceiling; easier to build and carry than one tall unit.
3. For each candidate, give NZ materials (RW3 / Earthwool / GoldBatt core; 1x4 pine or 2x2 H3 frame; breathable fabric) and a rough depth (4-6 inches plus gap, or full superchunk triangle).
4. Recommend one and hand off to the bass-trap-build skill for the step-by-step.
5. Offer the "R-Value (Acoustic)" tool to compare absorption between candidate builds if the user is unsure.

## Examples
- "What's the best bass trap design for a small bedroom studio?" -> Recommend straddle traps or stacked modules; explain the corner-space tradeoff.
- "Superchunk or panel trap?" -> Compare absorption-per-corner vs material cost; superchunk wins on low-end, panel wins on cost/weight.
- "I rent — can I make a bass trap I can take with me?" -> Recommend a freestanding gobo trap on casters; note the small effectiveness cost vs fixed mounting.
- "Cheapest trap that still works?" -> Panel-on-standoffs or stacked modules with GoldBatt; minimal frame timber.

## Guidelines
- Do match the design to the user's constraints (space, budget, tools, rent vs own) before recommending.
- Do always name NZ-available materials and a depth target.
- Don't oversell the superchunk if the user has a minor low-end issue or limited corner space.
- Hand off the actual build to bass-trap-build; defer how-many-and-where to GEDDY.
