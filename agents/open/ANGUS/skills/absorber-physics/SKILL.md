# Absorber Physics

ANGUS explains the why behind porous acoustic absorption so the user trusts the build decisions. The core idea: porous absorbers (Rockwool, Earthwool, GoldBatt) convert sound energy to heat through friction as air moves through the fibres. Absorption is strongest where air particle velocity is highest — a quarter wavelength away from a hard wall — which is exactly why depth and air gaps matter, and why corners (where multiple wall reflections pile up) are the prime real estate for low frequencies.

## Instructions
1. Anchor on the mechanism: sound is moving air; fibrous material resists that motion (airflow resistivity) and dissipates the energy as heat. Too dense = air can't enter; too sparse = no friction. Mid-density batts (RW3, Earthwool, GoldBatt) sit in the sweet spot.
2. Explain particle velocity: at a hard wall, air velocity is near zero (pressure max). Velocity peaks a quarter-wavelength out. Absorbers work on velocity, so material placed away from the wall (depth + air gap) catches lower frequencies.
3. Give the quarter-wavelength intuition: lower frequency = longer wavelength = the velocity peak is further from the wall = you need more depth (or a bigger air gap) to reach it. This is why a 2-inch panel does nothing for 60 Hz but a 6-inch corner trap with a gap does.
4. Explain corners: low-frequency energy concentrates at room boundaries and triples up at corners (three surfaces meet). A trap across the corner intercepts that concentrated energy and the triangular cavity behind it acts as extra effective depth.
5. Tie it back to the user's build: more depth and bigger air gaps buy lower-frequency control; density tunes the airflow resistance. Offer "R-Value (Acoustic)" to show the absorption curve.

## Examples
- "Why does my thin foam do nothing for bass?" -> Explain quarter-wavelength: thin material only reaches the velocity peak of high frequencies; bass needs depth.
- "Why an air gap behind the trap?" -> The gap moves the absorber toward the low-frequency velocity peak without using more insulation.
- "Why corners specifically?" -> Low-frequency pressure/energy concentrates at boundaries and stacks at corners; best bang per trap.
- "Can insulation be too dense?" -> Yes — too dense blocks airflow and reflects; mid-density batts let air move through and dissipate.
