# Album & EP Rollout Strategy

A rollout is the staged campaign that carries a body of work from finished masters to a successful release and a long tail. Good rollouts sequence assets, audience touchpoints, and platform mechanics so momentum compounds instead of peaking too early. This skill builds the full phased blueprint: pre-rollout setup, announcement, single cycles, release week, and post-release sustain.

## Instructions
1. Establish the fixed inputs first: the release date, the format (single / EP / album), the number of tracks, the lead single(s), and the artist's current audience size and primary platforms.
2. Work backwards from the release date to set a 6–10 week active rollout window (8 weeks is the default for an EP/album; compress to 3–4 for a single).
3. Define five phases and assign dates: (a) Pre-rollout / setup, (b) Announcement + pre-save live, (c) Single & content cycle, (d) Release week, (e) Post-release sustain.
4. In Pre-rollout: lock masters, artwork, metadata, splits, distribution upload (2–4 weeks ahead of date), pre-save/pre-add link, press kit / EPK, and a content bank (shoot teasers, BTS, visualizers in batch).
5. In Announcement: reveal the project (title, art, date), open pre-saves, send the first fan-list email, and brief any press/playlist contacts under embargo.
6. In the Single cycle: schedule lead single(s) ahead of the project so each acts as a discovery on-ramp; pair each with a content beat (teaser → release-day push → follow-up clip).
7. In Release week: front-load day-of activity — pre-save conversion, story takeovers, a pinned post, a fan email, day-2 and day-4 reinforcement so the streaming algorithm sees sustained engagement, not a single spike.
8. In Post-release: keep feeding the catalog for 4–8 weeks with alternate edits, live clips, sync/playlist outreach, and a recap to convert new listeners into followers.
9. Produce the output as a dated, phase-by-phase calendar with an owner and asset for every line item, plus a short "risks/dependencies" note (e.g. distributor lead time, pre-save link readiness).

## Examples
- "Plan the rollout for my 6-track EP dropping August 15." → Build an 8-week backward-dated calendar with all five phases, two single drops (week -4 and week -1), a batched content bank, and release-week day-by-day actions.
- "I only have 3 weeks before my single comes out — what do I do?" → Deliver a compressed single rollout: setup + pre-save now, teaser cycle across the weeks, and a tight release-week push; flag that distributor/pre-save lead time is the binding constraint.
- "My last album peaked on day one then died — fix my rollout." → Diagnose front-loading; rebuild with single on-ramps before release, sustained release-week beats (day 1/2/4), and a 6-week post-release sustain plan to flatten the decay curve.
- "What goes in the pre-rollout phase?" → Enumerate the setup checklist (masters, art, metadata, splits, distribution upload, pre-save, EPK, content bank) with lead-time targets.

## Guidelines
- Always anchor every recommendation to a date relative to release day; a rollout without a calendar is just a vibe.
- Default to building, not just listing — give the user a usable schedule with owners and assets.
- Do batch content production in pre-rollout; don't assume the artist can shoot week-to-week under release pressure.
- Don't let the whole campaign fire on day one. Sustained engagement signals beat single spikes for editorial and algorithmic playlisting.
- Right-size the window to audience and format: a developing artist with a single shouldn't run an 8-week album-scale campaign.
- Surface dependencies early — distributor lead time, pre-save link, split sheets, and metadata are the usual blockers.
- When format choice, distributor choice, or platform tactics come up, hand off to waterfall-strategy, distribution, or release-strategies rather than duplicating them here.
