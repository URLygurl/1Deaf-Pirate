# Waterfall vs. Traditional vs. Singles Strategy

The format you release in shapes how momentum builds. This skill helps an independent artist choose among three approaches — the waterfall (progressively bundling singles into a growing release), the traditional all-at-once drop, and a campaign of standalone singles — and then execute the chosen one, with special depth on the waterfall mechanics that many artists get wrong.

## Instructions
1. Diagnose the project: number of finished tracks, the artist's release cadence history, audience size, and whether the goal is sustained discovery or a single big moment.
2. Explain the three formats plainly:
   - **Waterfall:** release single 1, then re-release it bundled with single 2, then 1+2+3, etc., each new "release" carrying the prior tracks forward — so the final EP/album drops with its singles already attached and their streams consolidated.
   - **Traditional:** all tracks drop at once on release day as the complete project (usually after 1–3 lead singles released separately, not bundled).
   - **Singles campaign:** standalone singles released on a cadence with no bundling, treated as discrete songs.
3. Recommend waterfall when the artist wants each single to keep feeding a growing release, wants the album to launch with momentum already attached, and can commit to a disciplined multi-week cadence.
4. Recommend traditional when the work is meant to be experienced as a cohesive body, the artist has the audience to drive a big day-one moment, or the narrative arc matters more than staggered discovery.
5. Recommend a singles campaign when the artist is building a catalog and audience, isn't ready to commit tracks to an album, or wants maximum number of discovery on-ramps.
6. For a chosen waterfall: set the cadence (commonly every 2–4 weeks), confirm the distributor supports waterfall re-releases on the same release/ISRC handling, keep consistent artwork/credits across steps, and decide tracklist order to put the strongest discovery track first.
7. Flag the trade-offs: waterfall fragments release-day attention and demands sustained effort; traditional concentrates impact but risks a one-day spike; singles maximize on-ramps but can dilute the "project" narrative.
8. Output a clear recommendation with rationale, plus an execution outline (sequence, dates, and distributor requirements) for the selected format.

## Examples
- "Should I do a waterfall or just drop the whole EP?" → Compare both against the artist's track count, cadence ability, and audience; recommend one with rationale and an execution sketch.
- "Explain how a waterfall release actually works." → Walk through the bundle-forward mechanic step by step (single 1 → 1+2 → 1+2+3 → full EP) and why it consolidates streams and keeps momentum.
- "I have 5 songs and post inconsistently — what format?" → Likely steer away from waterfall (it demands disciplined cadence); suggest 1–2 lead singles into a traditional drop, or a paced singles campaign, with reasons.
- "How often should the waterfall steps go out?" → Recommend a 2–4 week cadence, tie it to the artist's content capacity, and note distributor and artwork-consistency requirements.

## Guidelines
- Make a recommendation — don't just list the three options and leave the choice hanging.
- Be honest about the waterfall's cost: it only pays off with sustained, disciplined execution; a flaky cadence kills its advantage.
- Confirm distributor support before committing to a waterfall; not all handle re-release bundling cleanly, and mishandled ISRCs/metadata can split streams instead of consolidating them.
- Keep artwork, credits, and metadata consistent across waterfall steps so the releases read as one growing project.
- Don't conflate "lead singles before a traditional album" with a waterfall — in traditional, the singles aren't re-bundled.
