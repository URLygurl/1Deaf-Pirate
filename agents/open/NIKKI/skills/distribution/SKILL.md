# Distribution

Distribution is the mechanical process of delivering finished music to streaming platforms and stores independently — selecting a distributor, preparing audio and artwork to spec, getting metadata and rights right, and scheduling delivery so editorial and release timing work. NIKKI uses this to make sure the release is technically clean and on every platform on time, so the marketing rollout has something solid to point at.

## Instructions
1. Choose a distributor matched to the artist's needs: flat-fee per-release vs. annual subscription vs. revenue-share; check store reach, payout speed, editorial pitch access, and support for features like waterfall bundles, splits, and pre-saves (e.g. DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby, Amuse, Symphonic, Ditto — recommend by fit, not brand loyalty).
2. Prepare audio to spec: master to required format (typically WAV, 16-bit/44.1kHz minimum, lossless preferred), confirm true-peak/loudness sanity, and verify track order.
3. Prepare artwork to spec: square, minimum 3000x3000 px, RGB, no unauthorized logos/URLs/explicit-rights violations that cause rejection.
4. Get metadata right — this is where releases break: exact artist name, track titles, featured artists in the correct field, genre, language, explicit flags, and consistent credits. Assign/confirm ISRCs (per track) and UPC/EAN (per release); let the distributor generate them if the artist has none.
5. Handle rights and royalties: register songwriter/publishing splits, set up collection (PRO/mechanical/neighboring rights where applicable), and confirm cover-song licensing if relevant.
6. Set the release date with a delivery buffer — submit at least 3–5 weeks ahead to keep editorial pitching (Spotify for Artists, Apple Music) viable; longer is safer.
7. Enable pre-save/pre-add and confirm the smart link resolves to all stores before announcing.
8. After delivery, verify the release appears correctly on each DSP, claim Spotify for Artists / Apple Music for Artists profiles, and confirm the live date and pitch status.

## Examples
- "Which distributor should I use for my first EP?" → Ask about budget, catalog plans, and feature needs; recommend a flat-fee or subscription option by fit and explain the tradeoffs.
- "What do I need before I deliver to Spotify?" → Walk through audio spec, 3000x3000 artwork, complete metadata, ISRC/UPC, rights/splits, and the 3–5 week lead time.
- "Do I need ISRC codes?" → Explain yes, one per track; the distributor can issue them; needed for tracking and royalties.

## Guidelines
- Recommend distributors by fit (cost model, reach, features), never by default brand preference.
- Treat metadata accuracy as critical — wrong artist-name fields, missing featured-artist fields, or inconsistent credits cause rejections and split streams.
- Always enforce the 3–5 week minimum delivery buffer for editorial; flag tight timelines hard.
- Ensure ISRC (per track) and UPC (per release) are assigned and rights/splits are registered before delivery.
- Verify the release post-delivery on every DSP and confirm artist-profile claims before release day.
