# AS/NZS 3000 Wiring Rules

AS/NZS 3000 (the "Wiring Rules") is the joint Australian/New Zealand Standard that sets the requirements for the design, construction, and verification of electrical installations. It is the technical reference electricians work to for safe wiring, earthing, protection, and testing, and in NZ/AU it carries legal weight as a means of compliance. This skill helps production people understand what the standard governs and what "compliant" means, so they can plan correctly and brief electricians — while leaving the actual design, installation, and certification to qualified workers.

## Instructions
1. Explain what AS/NZS 3000 is: the joint AU/NZ Standard for electrical installations covering design, selection of equipment, installation, and verification (testing) of low-voltage installations, applied across Australia and New Zealand and cited in legislation as a compliance pathway.
2. Summarise its scope at a high level: earthing and equipotential bonding, protection against electric shock, overcurrent and fault protection, isolation and switching, cable selection and current-carrying capacity, RCD requirements, and verification/testing before energising.
3. Highlight RCD requirements: the Wiring Rules drive the use of RCDs (typically 30 mA) on socket-outlets and many final subcircuits, especially relevant to stage and outdoor/temporary power where shock risk is high.
4. Connect it to temporary/event work: permanent installations follow AS/NZS 3000, while temporary supplies for construction and events are addressed by companion standards (e.g. AS/NZS 3001 for transportable/temporary installations) that build on the same principles — confirm the right standard applies to the situation.
5. Stress verification: the Wiring Rules require testing (earth continuity, insulation resistance, polarity, RCD operation) before an installation is energised and put into service. "Looks fine" is not compliance; tested-and-certified is.
6. Tie compliance to people: working to AS/NZS 3000 is the job of a registered/licensed electrician (in NZ, EWRB-registered — see nz-ewrb), who is also responsible for the relevant compliance/safety certification.
7. Be clear this skill explains the standard's intent and scope for planning and briefing; it is not a substitute for the standard's text, an electrician's judgement, or legal advice. Defer specifics and clause-level requirements to the current published Standard and a qualified worker.

## Examples
- User: "Does our temporary festival power need to meet AS/NZS 3000?" -> Yes in principle; permanent installs follow AS/NZS 3000 and temporary/event supplies follow companion standards (e.g. AS/NZS 3001) built on the same rules. A registered electrician designs, installs, tests, and certifies it.
- User: "What does AS/NZS 3000 say about RCDs on our stage outlets?" -> Explain the standard drives 30 mA RCD protection on socket-outlets and final subcircuits, which is exactly why stage/outdoor power must be RCD-protected; the electrician confirms the specific clause requirements.
- User: "Is my install compliant?" -> Compliance means designed and installed to AS/NZS 3000 AND verified by the required tests (earth continuity, insulation, polarity, RCD trip) and certified by a registered electrician — not a visual guess.
- User: "What standard do electricians wire to in New Zealand?" -> AS/NZS 3000, the joint AU/NZ Wiring Rules, alongside related standards for specific situations; in NZ the worker must be EWRB-registered and licensed.

## Guidelines
- DO describe AS/NZS 3000 as the governing technical standard for installations and explain it requires earthing, shock/overcurrent protection, RCDs, correct cable sizing, isolation, and pre-energising verification.
- DO point to companion standards for temporary/event supplies and to a registered electrician for design and certification.
- DON'T quote specific clause numbers or current-carrying capacity tables as authoritative from memory; defer to the current published Standard and the electrician.
- DON'T imply that reading the standard authorises an unqualified person to perform or certify electrical work.
- EDGE CASES: the Standard is periodically amended/republished, so always work to the current edition; jurisdictional variations and amendments exist between AU and NZ; temporary, hazardous-area, and specialised installations invoke additional standards.
- OUTPUT: explain what the standard requires at a planning level, what "compliant and verified" means, and that a registered/licensed electrician (EWRB in NZ) does and certifies the work. Pair with nz-ewrb for the licensing side.
