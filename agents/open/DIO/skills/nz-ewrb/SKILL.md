# NZ Electrical Workers Registration Board (EWRB)

In New Zealand, most electrical work ("prescribed electrical work") may only be done by a person who is registered and holds a current practising licence with the Electrical Workers Registration Board (EWRB), or who is supervised appropriately. This skill helps the user understand who is legally allowed to do electrical work, how to verify a worker's status on the public register, and where the legal line sits for stage and event power. The core message for production: plug-and-play within ratings is fine, but connecting supply, building/wiring distro, and energising installations is registered work — verify the person before they touch it.

## Instructions
1. Explain the EWRB's role: it is the statutory body that registers electrical workers and issues practising licences in NZ under the electrical-workers regulatory regime (Electricity Act and associated regulations), and maintains a public register.
2. Distinguish registration from a practising licence: a person must be both registered in the appropriate class AND hold a current practising licence to legally carry out prescribed electrical work unsupervised. Lapsed licence = not currently authorised.
3. Outline the main registration classes at a high level: Electrician, Electrical Installer, Electrical Service Technician, Electrical Appliance Serviceperson, and Line Mechanic, plus trainee/limited categories working under supervision. Match the task to the right class rather than assuming "an electrician covers everything."
4. Direct the user to verify any worker on the EWRB public register / online check before engaging them: confirm name, registration class, and a current practising licence covering the work.
5. Frame "prescribed electrical work": connecting to mains supply, installing/altering fixed wiring, building and connecting distribution, and similar are prescribed and require a registered+licensed worker. Some low-risk plug-connected tasks are not prescribed, but err toward caution on stage.
6. For events, advise that hiring an electrical contractor for temporary supply means checking both the individual's EWRB status and that the work will be certified/tested as required (Certificate of Compliance / Electrical Safety Certificate where applicable).
7. Be clear that this skill is regulatory guidance, not legal advice or authorisation: never suggest unqualified people perform prescribed electrical work, and refer users to the EWRB and a licensed contractor for the definitive position.

## Examples
- User: "How do I check my electrician is actually licensed in NZ?" -> Point them to the EWRB public register/online check, and tell them to confirm name, the correct registration class for the job, and a current (non-lapsed) practising licence.
- User: "Can the lighting tech wire our distro into the building supply at our NZ gig?" -> Only if they are EWRB-registered in the appropriate class with a current practising licence (or properly supervised); connecting to supply is prescribed electrical work. Otherwise, hire a registered electrician.
- User: "What's the difference between being registered and having a practising licence?" -> Registration is the qualification/class on the register; the practising licence is the current authorisation to actually do the work. Both are required to work legally and unsupervised.
- User: "We just want to plug our gear into existing outlets - do we need an electrician?" -> Plugging compliant gear into existing protected outlets within rating is generally fine; anything touching fixed wiring, supply connection, or distro build needs a registered, licensed worker.

## Guidelines
- DO direct users to verify registration class AND a current practising licence on the EWRB public register before anyone does electrical work.
- DO match the task to the correct registration class and flag when work is "prescribed electrical work."
- DON'T advise or imply that an unqualified or unlicensed person may perform prescribed electrical work, even informally or "just this once."
- DON'T present this as legal advice; the EWRB and a licensed contractor are the authoritative sources.
- EDGE CASES: trainees/limited registrations work only under supervision; an expired practising licence means the person is not currently authorised; overseas qualifications do not automatically grant NZ registration. Certification/testing (CoC/ESC) obligations sit with the registered worker.
- OUTPUT: tell the user exactly what to verify, where (EWRB register), and whether the task in question is prescribed work needing a registered+licensed person. Pair with the as-nzs-3000 skill for the technical standard that work must meet.
