# Tour Manager Role

A tour manager (TM) is the person responsible for the day-to-day running of a concert tour — handling logistics, finances, scheduling, advancing, and acting as the primary point of contact between the artist, the promoter, the venue, and the crew. The role blends operations, accounting, diplomacy, and problem-solving so the artist can focus on performing. This skill provides the knowledge to explain the role, structure responsibilities, and guide core TM workflows like advancing and settlement.

## Instructions
1. When explaining the role, frame the TM as the operational hub: they own the schedule, the money, the advance, and the communication chain between artist, crew, promoter, and venue.
2. Enumerate core responsibilities: advancing shows, building day sheets/itineraries, managing travel and accommodation, overseeing load-in/soundcheck/showtime timing, handling settlement and budgets, and managing crew and guest lists.
3. For advancing, walk the user through contacting each venue ahead of the date to confirm load-in times, stage and power specs, backline, hospitality, parking, curfew, and the rider — surfacing conflicts early.
4. For settlement, explain reconciling the deal (guarantee vs. door/percentage), expenses, taxes/withholding, and collecting payment at the end of the night.
5. For the day sheet, list what it contains: date/venue, addresses, key contacts, lobby/load-in/soundcheck/doors/set times, travel details, and notes.
6. Distinguish the TM from adjacent roles (production manager, stage manager, road manager) when scope questions arise, noting overlap on smaller tours where one person wears several hats.
7. Route document-creation requests to the appropriate technical skill rather than producing riders here.

## Examples
- "What does a tour manager actually do?" → Summarize the role as operational hub and list core responsibilities grouped by logistics, finance, and communication.
- "Walk me through advancing a show." → Give the advance checklist (times, specs, backline, hospitality, parking, curfew, rider) and explain how to surface conflicts early with the promoter.
- "What's a settlement?" → Explain reconciling the deal, expenses, and withholding, and collecting payment at end of night.

## Guidelines
- Be concrete and checklist-driven; touring people want actionable lists, not theory.
- Clarify role boundaries when asked, but acknowledge that on small tours one person often covers TM, production, and road duties.
- Do not produce technical documents in this skill — hand stage plots/input lists, backline, and full riders to their dedicated skills.
- Flag finance/settlement specifics (tax withholding, currency, per-territory rules) as situational and recommend confirming with the artist's accountant or the promoter.
