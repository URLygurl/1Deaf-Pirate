# Backline Rider

A backline rider lists the musical equipment behind the band — guitar and bass amplifiers, drum kits, keyboards, stands, and stools — that the promoter, venue, or rental house is expected to provide, typically for fly dates, festivals, or shared-bill shows where the act cannot carry its own gear. Precision here prevents the classic disaster of a touring drummer arriving to a kit with the wrong sizes, missing hardware, or a dead amp.

## Instructions
1. Confirm the context: is this a full provided-backline request (fly date/festival) or a partial supplement to gear the band carries? Scope the list accordingly.
2. Specify guitar/bass amplification by make and model with acceptable alternates, including head + cabinet vs. combo, wattage, and number of channels. Always give a primary and at least one acceptable substitute.
3. Spec the drum kit fully: kick size, rack/floor tom sizes, snare, all hardware (hi-hat stand, snare stand, cymbal stands count, kick pedal, throne), and whether cymbals and the snare are band-supplied. State preferred head brand if it matters.
4. Spec keyboards/synths by exact model with acceptable alternates, plus stand type (single/double tier), sustain pedal, and bench.
5. List small but critical items: guitar/bass stands, amp power, music stands, and any required adapters or transformers for international voltage.
6. Mark each item BAND-SUPPLIED vs. PROVIDED so responsibility is unambiguous.
7. Add contact and advance notes: who to confirm rentals with, deadline to confirm, and a line that substitutions must be approved in advance by the tour manager.

## Examples
- "Write a backline rider for a festival fly date, 4-piece." → Produce make/model amp specs with alternates, a full drum kit spec with hardware list, keyboard spec, and a BAND vs. PROVIDED column, plus an advance-confirmation deadline.
- "The promoter can't get the requested amp — what are acceptable subs?" → Offer tier-appropriate alternates in the same family/class and note which tonal trade-offs the artist should expect.
- "Add a backup amp head to the rider." → Insert a spare head line item, mark it PROVIDED, and note it should be patched and ready at FOH-side or stage-right.

## Guidelines
- Never list a single model with no alternate; promoters in different markets stock different gear, so always give acceptable substitutes.
- Be exhaustive on drum hardware — missing a hi-hat clutch or kick pedal is the most common failure.
- Call out voltage/power and international adapter needs for fly dates explicitly.
- State that all substitutions require advance approval, and give a confirmation deadline.
- Keep this rider gear-only; route mic and DI requirements to stage-plot-input-list and hospitality terms to rider-template.
- Output as a clean itemized list with a responsibility column, ready to email a promoter. Canonical reference: https://tourmanager.info/backline-rider/
