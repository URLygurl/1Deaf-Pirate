# Tube Amp Maintenance

This skill covers the routine care that keeps a tube guitar amp healthy and reliable, and it draws a hard safety line at the chassis. Owner-level maintenance is all external: inspecting and swapping tubes, always running a proper speaker load, replacing fuses like-for-like, keeping the amp clean and ventilated, and recognizing the signs of a worn or failing tube. **Tube amps store LETHAL voltages in their filter capacitors that remain charged for a long time even after the amp is unplugged.** Anything that requires opening the chassis — recapping, power-tube biasing, internal repair, cap discharge — belongs to a qualified amp technician, full stop.

## Instructions
1. Lead with safety: confirm the user will keep the chassis closed. State that filter caps hold lethal charge even when unplugged, so internal work and biasing go to a qualified tech.
2. Always run a speaker load. Tell the user never to power on a tube amp without a correctly-rated, connected speaker/cab using a proper unshielded speaker cable (not an instrument cable) — running without a load can destroy the output transformer. Match the amp's output impedance to the cab.
3. Warm-up and cool-down habits: use the standby switch to let the tubes warm before bringing them up to full power, and let the amp cool before moving it. Power tubes run hot; avoid bumps and drops while hot.
4. Inspect tubes periodically with the amp OFF, UNPLUGGED, and fully COOL. Look for a tube that won't glow, glows cherry-red on the plates (red-plating = a problem, power down), or is cracked or has turned white/milky inside (lost vacuum — dead).
5. Identify a microphonic or worn tube: tapping a suspect tube (amp on, careful, at low volume) produces ringing/squeal through the speaker. Worn tubes cause loss of volume, fizzy or muddy tone, excess hiss, or intermittent crackle. Swapping a like-for-like preamp tube (e.g. 12AX7) is owner-safe.
6. Power tubes are different: replacing power tubes (e.g. EL34, 6L6, EL84) usually requires re-biasing — treat retubing of power tubes and bias adjustment as a tech procedure, not DIY (unless the amp is explicitly self-biasing/cathode-biased and the manual says otherwise).
7. Fuses: replace only with the exact rating and type printed at the holder. A fuse that blows again immediately signals an internal fault — stop and see a tech.
8. Keep it clean and ventilated: wipe dust from the chassis exterior and vents, keep the amp out of damp/extreme-heat environments, and don't block the ventilation slots. Recommend a cover when stored.
9. Service intervals: typical preamp tubes last a long time; power tubes commonly want replacement roughly every 1-2 years of regular gigging or when symptoms appear. Recommend a periodic tech check-up (caps, bias, jacks) for older amps.

## Examples
- User: "How do I take care of my tube amp so it lasts?" → Give the external routine: always use a speaker load, standby warm-up, keep it clean/ventilated, inspect tubes when cool, fuses like-for-like; note bias/recap is a tech job.
- User: "My power tubes are glowing bright red." → Red-plating; have them power down immediately. It indicates a biasing or tube fault — refer to a qualified tech rather than continuing to play.
- User: "Should I retube my amp myself?" → Like-for-like preamp tube swaps are owner-safe; power-tube replacement needs re-biasing and goes to a tech. Warn about lethal cap voltage if they're tempted to open the chassis.
- User: "Can I test my amp without the speaker plugged in?" → No — running a tube amp without a load can destroy the output transformer; always have a correctly-rated speaker connected with a speaker cable.

## Guidelines
- ALWAYS state the lethal-voltage caveat for any chassis-internal topic and refuse to give cap-discharge or biasing instructions; redirect to a qualified technician.
- Do insist on a connected, impedance-matched speaker load whenever the amp is powered, using a speaker cable rather than an instrument cable.
- Do inspect and swap tubes only when the amp is off, unplugged, and cool; tubes get burn-hot.
- Don't treat power-tube replacement or biasing as DIY; don't uprate or bypass fuses.
- Edge case: red-plating, internal arcing, smoke, or a fuse that blows on replacement = power down, unplug, and go to a tech immediately.
- Output: a clear maintenance checklist, the likely status of the user's tubes, and an explicit owner-safe vs. tech-required verdict for any repair.
