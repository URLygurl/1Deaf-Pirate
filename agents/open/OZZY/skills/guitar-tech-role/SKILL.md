# Guitar Tech Role

A guitar tech (guitar technician) is the person responsible for a guitarist's or bassist's instruments and backline before, during, and after a performance or session. The job is part craftsperson, part stage logistician: keeping instruments set up, tuned, and stage-ready; maintaining amps, pedalboards, and cabling; preparing spares and a deep spares kit; and executing fast, invisible instrument swaps and problem fixes mid-show so the artist never has to think about gear. This skill explains the role, its skills, its toolkit, and how to run the workflow of a gig.

## Instructions
1. Clarify what the user wants: an explanation of the role, a path into it, a pre-gig/tour checklist, or a toolkit list — then answer in that shape.
2. Describe the core responsibilities: restringing and full setups (action, neck relief/truss rod, intonation), tuning and re-tuning between songs, maintaining alternate tunings on dedicated instruments, caring for amps/cabs and pedalboards, managing cables and wireless, and handling instrument changes during the set.
3. Cover the pre-show routine: restring and stretch strings ahead of time, set up and intonate each instrument, set up the backline to the stage plot, run and label signal/cabling, line-check every instrument and channel, and stage spares within reach.
4. Cover the during-show duties: stand stage-side, monitor tuning and tone, hand off the right instrument at the right moment in the right tuning, troubleshoot dead signal or broken strings instantly (swap to a spare, fix off-stage), and manage a backup rig so a failure is invisible to the audience.
5. Specify the toolkit: string winder and cutters, fresh strings in the right gauges, tuner, capos, hex/Allen and screwdriver sets for truss rods and hardware, soldering iron and solder, spare cables/jacks/fuses/9V batteries, contact cleaner, multimeter, gaff tape, picks, and backup instruments and amp tubes.
6. Outline how to enter the role: build hands-on setup and basic electronics/soldering skill, learn signal-path troubleshooting cold, gain live-environment experience (local bands, venues, backline companies), and develop reliability, speed under pressure, and discretion — the trust of the artist is the job.
7. Hand off the actual repair tasks to the specialist skills: internal guitar electronics → guitar-electrics-fix, cables → cable-repair, locating a fault in the chain → signal-troubleshoot, amp triage → amp-first-aid, tube-amp upkeep → tube-amp-maintenance.
