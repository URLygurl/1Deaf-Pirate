# Road Case Primer (Practical)

This skill walks the user through specifying and building a protective road/flight case for touring or shipping gear. A good case absorbs shock, resists crush and moisture, survives baggage/freight handling, and is exactly the right size — too loose lets gear rattle, too tight lets shock transfer straight in. The classic ATA-style case is plywood panels, extruded aluminum edge frame, riveted ball corners, recessed butterfly latches, spring/dish handles, and foam interior.

## Instructions
1. Measure the gear precisely (LxWxH) including knobs, connectors, and protrusions; add clearance for foam — typically ~2.5 cm (1") of foam on every face for shock protection.
2. Choose the shell: 9–12 mm laminated/phenolic plywood for ATA cases (light, tough), or rotomolded/injection plastic and aluminum for harsher duty. Match weight budget to airline/freight limits.
3. Spec the hardware: extruded aluminum tongue-and-groove edge frame, steel ball corners, recessed spring-loaded butterfly latches, recessed spring/strap handles, and edge "tour-grade" castle/coffin nuts where abuse is expected.
4. Add casters for anything heavy/rolling: 4" swivel casters (two with brakes), mounted on a reinforced caster board; keep total rolling height/weight manageable.
5. Pick foam by job:
   - **Convoluted (egg-crate)** foam for lids and light cushioning.
   - **Charcoal/ester PU foam** for general padding and pick-and-pluck custom cavities.
   - **Cross-linked PE / Ethafoam** (denser) for heavy items and load-bearing supports.
   Use closed-cell denser foam to bear weight, softer foam to cushion.
6. Design for handling: balance the load, mark "this way up," size for two-person lift or add casters, and leave cable/accessory pockets only if they don't compromise protection.
7. Decide buy vs build: off-the-shelf rack/utility cases for standard gear; custom for odd shapes or high value. Provide a parts list if building.

## Examples
- User: "Spec a flight case for a 4U rack of wireless gear." → 4U rack-mount case with rackrails, ~2 RU vent/cable clearance, ball corners, butterfly latches, recessed handles, foam-lined lid.
- User: "What foam protects a fragile tube amp?" → Soft ester PU pick-and-pluck cavity for the chassis, denser cross-linked PE blocks under load points, 1" minimum on all faces.
- User: "Build vs buy for a 15-inch PA top?" → If it's a standard model, a molded/utility case; if custom-shaped, build an ATA plywood case to measured dimensions.
- User: "My gear rattles in its case." → Cavity is oversized; add denser foam to remove play without crushing connectors.

## Guidelines
- Foam clearance and density are the whole game: cushion fragile faces, support heavy ones.
- Recessed hardware only — proud latches/handles get sheared off in transit.
- Respect airline ATA-300 expectations for fly dates: rugged shell, ball corners, foam.
- Watch total weight; an indestructible case nobody can lift is a failed case.
