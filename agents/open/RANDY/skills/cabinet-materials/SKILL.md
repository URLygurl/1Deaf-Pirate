# Cabinet Materials (Baltic Birch vs MDF and Beyond)

This skill guides material, panel-thickness, bracing, and joinery decisions for loudspeaker cabinets. The two workhorse materials are MDF (cheap, dense, dead, easy to machine, heavy, moisture-fragile) and Baltic birch plywood (stiff, strong, lighter for its stiffness, screw-holding, more expensive, can ring more). The right choice depends on whether the box lives in a hi-fi rack or gets thrown in a truck.

## Instructions
1. Pick the material by use case:
   - **MDF** — home/studio cabinets where weight doesn't travel and cost matters; excellent self-damping and flat surfaces for veneer/paint. Avoid where it'll get wet or dropped.
   - **Baltic birch (multi-ply, void-free)** — touring, PA, guitar/bass cabs, and anywhere strength-to-weight and screw retention matter; takes hardware and abuse far better.
   - Mention plywood grade: true Baltic/Russian birch is void-free, many thin plies; avoid cheap construction ply with voids.
2. Set panel thickness: ~18 mm (3/4") is the default for most cabs; 12 mm (1/2") for small/portable; 25 mm+ or doubled panels for large subs. Thicker isn't a substitute for bracing.
3. Brace to break up panel resonances: window/shelf braces, cross-bracing, or a matrix for large panels; aim to subdivide spans and tie opposing walls. Bracing beats raw thickness for killing panel "talk."
4. Choose joinery for airtightness and strength: rabbet/dado or lock-miter joints, butt joints with glue blocks, glued with PVA (wood glue) plus screws/clamps; the box must be airtight for sealed/ported tuning to hold.
5. Add damping/stuffing appropriately: line walls with felt/foam for reflections; stuff sealed boxes with polyfill (raises effective Vb slightly); use stuffing sparingly in ported boxes (not in the port path).
6. Seal MDF edges and faces (it drinks moisture and paint); finish birch with veneer, paint, or tough coatings for road use.
7. Weigh the weight: estimate panel weight early for portable/touring builds; birch saves meaningful mass over MDF at equal stiffness.

## Examples
- User: "MDF or Baltic birch for a bass guitar cab I gig with?" → Baltic birch: lighter, survives transport, holds screws and corner hardware; MDF would be heavy and crack-prone.
- User: "How thick and how much bracing for a 12-inch sub?" → Suggest 18 mm birch or MDF with a window brace tying front-to-back and a shelf brace; double the baffle if budget allows.
- User: "My cabinet panels buzz." → Diagnose under-bracing; add cross-braces and damping, check for an air leak at a joint.
- User: "Cheapest dead material for a studio monitor box?" → MDF, 18 mm, sealed joints, veneer or sealed-and-painted.

## Guidelines
- Airtight beats thick: a leaky joint ruins sealed/ported tuning regardless of material.
- Don't oversize thickness to avoid bracing — bracing is more weight-efficient.
- Warn that bare MDF and moisture/outdoor use don't mix.
- Match fasteners to material: pilot-drill MDF to avoid blowout; birch holds screws directly.
