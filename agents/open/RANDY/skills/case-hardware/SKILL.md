# Road-Case Hardware

A road case lives or dies by its hardware. This skill specs the parts that take the abuse so the gear doesn't: latches, handles, corners, casters, edge extrusion, and the rivets that hold it together. RANDY builds to the ATA 300 Category I / touring convention — recessed dish hardware, ball corners, butterfly latches, on a riveted aluminium-extrusion-and-plywood carcass. Millimetres throughout.

## ATA 300 baseline
ATA Spec 300 Category I is the reusable air-freight container standard road cases target. Practical hallmarks RANDY builds to:
- Recessed (dish/spring) handles so nothing protrudes to snag or shear off.
- Recessed twist/butterfly latches, dished into the panel — flush profile, positive lock.
- Ball corners (stackable steel) on every corner; they take the drop and let cases stack/interlock.
- Riveted construction with aluminium edge extrusion capturing 9-12 mm birch ply panels.
- Tongue-and-groove (male/female) extrusion at the lid joint so the lid registers and seals.

## Instructions
1. Identify the case role: rack, cabinet/utility, or fragile-gear case — it sets handle, caster, and foam choices.
2. Spec latches: recessed **butterfly (twist) latches** as default for touring; size and count to the lid weight (two on a small lid, more for long/heavy lids). Add a hasp/padlockable latch where security matters.
3. Spec handles: **recessed spring-loaded dish handles** so the case stacks flat and nothing protrudes. One per end minimum; surface "strap" handles only on light cases.
4. Spec corners: **stackable ball corners** on all 8 corners; medium ball for typical cases, large/heavy-duty for big or heavily loaded cases. Ball corners enable stacking and interlock.
5. Spec edge trim: aluminium **edge extrusion** (angle or tongue-and-groove "hybrid") riveted over the panel edges; tongue-and-groove at the lid mating line for registration and a tighter seal.
6. Spec casters (if mobile): bolt-through plate casters on a reinforced caster board, braked on at least two; 80-100 mm for utility, larger for heavy racks. Never rivet-only mount casters — through-bolt with backing.
7. Fastening: **rivets** (steel/aluminium) for hardware-to-extrusion; through-bolts with washers/backing plates for load-bearing parts (casters, heavy handles).
8. Call dimensions and counts in millimetres; match hardware load rating to the packed weight, not the empty case.

## Examples
- User: "Spec hardware for a touring rack case on wheels." — Recessed butterfly latches, recessed dish handles each end, 8 stackable ball corners, aluminium tongue-and-groove edge extrusion, through-bolted braked plate casters on a reinforced caster board. ATA 300 profile.
- User: "Butterfly latch or recessed draw latch?" — Both recess flush; butterfly/twist latches give a positive cam lock and are the touring default. Use a padlockable/hasp version where security is needed.
- User: "What corners survive being dropped off a stage?" — Stackable steel ball corners on all corners; they absorb the impact and let cases interlock when stacked. Upsize to heavy-duty ball corners for big/heavy cases.
- User: "Why recessed handles instead of strap handles?" — Recessed dish handles sit flush so cases stack flat and the handle can't snag, shear, or get crushed in freight — the ATA 300 reason.

## Guidelines
- Default to the ATA 300 recessed/ball-corner/butterfly profile for anything that tours or flies.
- Through-bolt load-bearing hardware (casters, heavy handles) with backing plates; rivet the rest into the extrusion.
- Match every part's load rating to packed weight, with margin — under-rated casters and corners fail first.
- Don't surface-mount protruding hardware on touring cases; it snags and shears.
- Edge case: very heavy cases need a reinforced caster board and possibly recessed caster cups; say so rather than bolting casters to a thin base.
- Output expectation: latch/handle/corner/extrusion/caster call with counts and millimetre sizes, fastening method, tied to the case role and ATA 300.
