# Road Case Theory

This skill provides the conceptual grounding for road/flight cases: what they are, the standards they meet, the named parts, and the categories. A road case (a.k.a. flight case) is a rugged shipping/transport container built to protect equipment during the repeated rough handling of touring and freight. The canonical ATA-style case answers a simple question — how do you get fragile, expensive gear to the next city intact, every night.

## Instructions
1. Define the object: a reusable protective container, typically a plywood shell bound by an extruded aluminum edge frame, reinforced with steel corners and recessed hardware, lined with foam.
2. Cover the standard: **ATA Spec 300** (Air Transport Association) defines category ratings for shipping containers; "ATA-rated" cases meet construction/durability expectations for air freight and many one-trip-vs-many-trip categories. Note it's a durability/construction spec, not a single legal mandate.
3. Name the anatomy:
   - **Edge/extrusion frame** — aluminum channel binding panel edges.
   - **Ball corners** — steel domed corners that take impact and stack loads.
   - **Butterfly/draw latches** — recessed cam latches; **spring/dish handles** — recessed grips.
   - **Caster dish/board** — reinforced base for wheels.
   - **Tongue-and-groove / valance** — the lid-to-base sealing joint.
   - **Foam interior** — convoluted, pick-and-pluck, or load-bearing PE.
4. Distinguish the categories: **rack cases** (19" rackmount gear), **trunk/utility cases**, **shock-mount cases** (floating inner shell on foam/elastomer for sensitive electronics), **hardware/work-box cases**, **coffin cases** (low, wide for guitars/keys), **shipping vs touring** builds.
5. Clarify terminology overlap: "road case" and "flight case" are largely interchangeable; "anvil case" references a major maker; "fly date" means gear travels by air and needs airline-grade cases.
6. Explain the design rationale when asked: recessed hardware survives drops, ball corners distribute impact and enable stacking, foam decouples shock, the aluminum frame ties panels into a rigid box.

## Examples
- User: "What's the difference between a road case and a flight case?" → Largely the same thing; "flight case" emphasizes air-travel/ATA-grade ruggedness, "road case" the touring context.
- User: "What does ATA-300 actually mean?" → A construction/durability spec from the Air Transport Association with category ratings for how many trips a container should survive.
- User: "Name the parts of a road case." → Walk the anatomy: edge frame, ball corners, butterfly latches, recessed handles, caster board, valance, foam.
- User: "Why recessed handles?" → So nothing protrudes to be sheared off or to interfere with stacking during rough handling.
