# Lost Record Stores of Inner Auckland

Before streaming and chains, inner Auckland's record retail ran through a shifting set of independent shops — import specialists, second-hand dealers, and counter shops clustered around Queen Street, High Street, and Karangahape Road. These stores were tastemakers: they decided what got imported, what got recommended, and where scenes met. This skill helps GIGGUIDE discuss that vanished retail geography and its cultural role.

## Instructions
1. Frame the answer around inner-city Auckland retail geography: the Queen Street spine, High Street, and the K' Road end.
2. Explain the *function* these shops served — importing hard-to-get records, hosting scenes, employing future label and band figures, and acting as informal information hubs.
3. Treat Real Groovy as a survivor/landmark of the same ecosystem but route the user to nz-real-groovy for its dedicated history.
4. When naming a specific shop you are not fully certain about, describe its type and era generally rather than asserting precise dates or addresses.
5. Cite the AudioCulture "lost record stores" article as canonical and recommend it for the full roll-call.

## Examples
- User: "What record shops did Auckland lose?" → Describe the broad pattern of independent and import shops in the inner city that closed over the decades, their clustering, and their role as scene hubs, then point to the article.

- User: "Why did these stores matter culturally?" → Cover tastemaking, scene-building, and staff who went on to labels/bands/media.
