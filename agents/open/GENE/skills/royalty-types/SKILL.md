# Royalty Types

This is GENE's catalog of the money streams a piece of music generates. A song earns from several distinct royalty types, each triggered by a different use, each collected by a different body, and each split across the two copyrights (composition and master). The goal is to give the user a complete checklist so no stream goes unclaimed. Canonical reference: https://royaltyexchange.com/blog/understanding-music-royalty-types-a-beginners-guide-2025

## Instructions

1. List the core royalty types and what triggers each:
   - **Performance royalties** — earned when a **composition** is performed publicly: terrestrial radio, TV, venues, bars, and streaming. Collected by **PROs** (ASCAP/BMI/SESAC/GMR) and paid as a writer share and a publisher share.
   - **Mechanical royalties** — earned when a **composition** is **reproduced**: physical copies, downloads, and interactive streams. In the US collected for streaming/downloads by **The MLC**; physical/sync mechanicals often via the label or Harry Fox.
   - **Sync (synchronization) royalties** — earned when music is paired with **visual media** (film, TV, ads, games, social). Requires licensing **both** the composition (sync license, from publisher/writer) and the master (master-use license, from label/artist); fees are negotiated, not statutory.
   - **Print royalties** — earned from printed/digital **sheet music** and lyric reproductions of the composition.
   - **Master-side digital performance / neighboring rights** — earned when a **sound recording** is played on non-interactive digital and satellite radio (e.g., internet radio, SiriusXM). In the US collected by **SoundExchange**, which pays the master owner, the featured artist, and non-featured performers.
2. Always tag each royalty to its **copyright** (composition vs master) and its **collector** so the user knows where to register to capture it.
3. Distinguish **interactive** streaming (on-demand, e.g., Spotify play you choose) — which generates both performance and mechanical on the composition plus master royalties — from **non-interactive** digital radio, which generates the master-side SoundExchange royalty.
4. Give the user a registration checklist to actually capture everything: PRO (performance), The MLC (mechanical), SoundExchange (master digital performance), distributor/label (master sales/streaming), and a publishing admin for sync and global collection.
5. Call out commonly missed streams: SoundExchange (artists who never register leave master digital-radio money unclaimed), foreign/neighboring rights, and YouTube Content ID / micro-sync income.
6. When the user names a specific platform or use, decompose it into the royalty types it triggers and route each to its collector.
7. State that this is general music-business education, not legal or financial advice; exact rates, splits, and the right administration setup depend on the user's deals and territories.

## Examples

- User: "What royalties does a Spotify stream generate?" -> An interactive stream pays on the **composition** (performance via PRO + mechanical via The MLC) and on the **master** (artist/label streaming royalty via the distributor). Three pipes from one play — make sure all are set up.
- User: "A YouTuber wants to use my song in a video." -> That's a **sync**: they need a sync license for your composition and a master-use license for your recording. Fees are negotiated. If they don't license but the song appears, Content ID can still recover some income.
- User: "What is SoundExchange and do I need it?" -> It collects the **master-side digital performance royalty** for non-interactive/satellite radio (SiriusXM, internet radio). If you're a featured or non-featured performer or master owner, register — this money is unclaimed by default.
- User: "List every way my single can earn." -> Performance (PRO), mechanical (MLC), master streaming/sales (distributor), master digital performance (SoundExchange), sync (negotiated, both copyrights), print (sheet music), plus foreign/neighboring rights and Content ID. Give it as a checklist tagged by copyright and collector.

## Guidelines

- Always tag each royalty type with its copyright (composition vs master) and its collector — the user's goal is knowing where to register.
- Do provide a concrete registration checklist; an unregistered stream pays nothing.
- Do separate interactive from non-interactive streaming, since they trigger different royalties.
- Don't present sync or print as automatic/statutory — sync is negotiated per deal.
- Don't imply a single signup captures everything; capturing all streams takes multiple registrations.
- Edge case: territories matter — foreign performance/mechanical and neighboring rights often require a publishing administrator or CMO membership abroad to collect.
- Output: a tagged list (royalty type -> copyright -> trigger -> collector) and an actionable registration checklist, ending with the not-legal-advice caveat.
