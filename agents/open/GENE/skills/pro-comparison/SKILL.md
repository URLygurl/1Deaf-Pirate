# PRO Comparison: ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, GMR

A Performing Rights Organization licenses the public performance right in **compositions** and collects **performance royalties** when songs are streamed, broadcast on radio/TV, or played in public venues — then distributes them to the affiliated songwriters and publishers. This skill helps GENE guide a writer through picking a US PRO. The big practical fact: every songwriter should be affiliated with exactly one PRO, because that single affiliation is how performance money finds them at all. Canonical reference: https://soundcharts.com/en/blog/bmi-vs-ascap

## Instructions

1. Define the PRO's job narrowly: it collects **public performance royalties for the composition** and pays the **writer share** and the **publisher share** separately. It does **not** collect mechanicals, sync fees you negotiate directly, or any master/SoundExchange money — set that boundary immediately.
2. Cover the four US PROs:
   - **ASCAP** — member-owned, not-for-profit; open to all; historically a modest one-time membership fee (separate writer and publisher accounts); board includes elected members.
   - **BMI** — open to all; historically free to join as a songwriter. Now operates **for-profit** after its 2024 acquisition, a change worth flagging because it affects how the company is run and how royalties may be handled.
   - **SESAC** — **invitation-only**, for-profit; you cannot simply sign up; smaller, more selective roster.
   - **GMR** (Global Music Rights) — **invitation-only**, for-profit boutique founded by Irving Azoff; very selective, high-profile catalog.
3. Explain the one-PRO rule: a writer affiliates with a **single** PRO for their writer share at any given time; you don't double-register the same works across PROs. (A publishing *entity* can have matching affiliations, but keep this simple unless asked.)
4. Frame the real decision: for most new/independent writers it's **ASCAP vs BMI** because SESAC and GMR are invite-only. Compare on signup cost, payout cadence/methodology, distribution timing, catalog fit, and member services — and note these terms change over time, so confirm current details on each PRO's site before deciding.
5. Tie it back to the bigger picture: joining a PRO captures the **performance** slice only. Remind the user they still need The MLC (mechanicals) and SoundExchange (master digital performance) to collect everything — PRO affiliation alone is not "set up to get paid."
6. Note the publisher dimension: writers signed to or self-administering a publisher will have both a writer account and a publisher account at the PRO; the PRO splits performance income into those two shares.
7. State that this is general industry education, not legal/financial advice — affiliation agreements and any publishing deal terms should be read carefully and, for catalog of value, reviewed by a music attorney.

## Examples

- User: "Should I join ASCAP or BMI?" -> Explain both are open to all; ASCAP is member-owned/not-for-profit with a small fee, BMI is now for-profit (post-2024) and historically free. Compare payout timing and services, tell them to pick **one**, and to verify current fees/terms on each site.
- User: "How do I join SESAC?" -> You generally can't just apply — SESAC is **invitation-only**, as is GMR. For an independent writer the realistic options are ASCAP or BMI.
- User: "I'm with ASCAP — am I all set to collect royalties?" -> Only for **performance** royalties on your compositions. You still need The MLC for mechanicals and SoundExchange for master digital-performance royalties; PRO affiliation is one of three registrations, not all of them.
- User: "Can I put my songs in both ASCAP and BMI?" -> No — affiliate your writer share with a single PRO. Splitting the same works across PROs creates conflicts and misrouted payments.

## Guidelines

- Always scope the PRO to **performance royalties on the composition** and explicitly say what it does NOT cover (mechanicals, sync, master royalties).
- Do flag that BMI is now for-profit (2024) and that SESAC and GMR are invitation-only — these are the facts that most change the user's options.
- Do tell users to verify current fee and distribution details on each PRO's website, since terms evolve.
- Don't recommend joining multiple PROs for the same writer share.
- Don't let PRO signup be mistaken for "fully registered to get paid" — always cross-reference mechanicals and master collection.
- Edge case: international writers join their home-country society (e.g., PRS, SOCAN, GEMA), which has reciprocal agreements with US PROs; don't push a non-US writer into a US PRO.
- Output: a short comparison framed around the realistic ASCAP-vs-BMI choice for independents, the invite-only note for SESAC/GMR, the one-PRO rule, and the not-legal-advice caveat.
