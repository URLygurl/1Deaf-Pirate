# Mechanical Royalties

A mechanical royalty is owed to the owners of a **musical composition** every time that composition is **reproduced** — pressed to a physical product (CD, vinyl), sold as a permanent download, or delivered as an interactive/on-demand stream. It is a composition-side royalty (songwriter + publisher), entirely separate from the master/recording royalty and from performance royalties. The name comes from the old "mechanical" reproduction of music; the right is now governed in the US largely by the compulsory (statutory) license. Canonical reference: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_royalties

## Instructions

1. Define it precisely: a mechanical is the **reproduction** royalty on the **composition**. Distinguish it sharply from (a) performance royalties (public play, via PROs) and (b) master royalties (sale/stream of the specific recording, to artist/label).
2. Explain the US **compulsory mechanical license** (Section 115 of the Copyright Act): once a composition has been commercially released with the writer's permission, anyone may record and release their own cover of it without asking, provided they pay the statutory mechanical rate. This is why you don't need permission to cover a song — but you do owe mechanicals.
3. Cover the **statutory rate** for physical and downloads: historically **9.1 cents** per copy for songs five minutes or under (and a per-minute rate for longer songs), set by the Copyright Royalty Board. Note rates are adjusted over time, so the user should confirm the current figure rather than treat 9.1¢ as permanent.
4. Cover **streaming mechanicals** separately: interactive streams generate a mechanical computed by a formula (a percentage-of-revenue / per-stream calculation set by the CRB), not the flat physical penny rate. In the US these streaming/download mechanicals are collected and paid by **The MLC** (the Mechanical Licensing Collective), created by the Music Modernization Act.
5. Name the collectors clearly: **The MLC** for US streaming/download mechanicals; **Harry Fox Agency (HFA)** historically for physical and licensing administration; and abroad, the local mechanical/collecting society (e.g., MCPS, etc.).
6. Stress the self-release trap: an artist who writes and records their own song still must register the **composition** with The MLC (and a PRO for performance) — the distributor pays the master streaming royalty but does NOT automatically pay the writer's mechanical; unregistered, that money accrues unmatched.
7. Walk a cover scenario: the person releasing a cover owes mechanicals on the underlying composition (via the compulsory license / The MLC), while owning and collecting their own master separately.
8. State plainly this is general music-business education, not legal advice — exact rates, formulas, and filings change and should be verified, with a music attorney/administrator for anything of value.

## Examples

- User: "Do I need permission to release a cover song?" -> Under the US compulsory license (Section 115) you generally don't need permission once the song's been commercially released, but you DO owe mechanical royalties to the songwriter/publisher, paid via the statutory license / The MLC. (Sync to video is different and is NOT compulsory.)
- User: "What's the mechanical rate?" -> For physical/downloads it's historically 9.1¢ per copy (≤5 min), with a longer-song per-minute rate; streaming mechanicals use a separate CRB revenue formula, not the penny rate. Confirm the current figures since they're periodically reset.
- User: "I self-released — am I collecting my mechanicals?" -> Not automatically. Your distributor pays the master streaming royalty; the composition's mechanical comes via The MLC, which you must register your works with. Many writers leave this money unclaimed.
- User: "Who is The MLC vs Harry Fox?" -> The MLC collects/pays US streaming and download mechanicals under the Music Modernization Act; Harry Fox historically handled physical mechanical licensing and administration.

## Guidelines

- Always anchor a mechanical to **reproduction of the composition** — never let it blur into master sales or performance royalties.
- Do explain that covers are allowed without permission under the compulsory license but still owe mechanicals; sync uses, by contrast, are not compulsory and must be negotiated.
- Do treat the 9.1¢ figure as a historical/illustrative physical rate and tell the user to verify the current statutory rate and streaming formula.
- Don't tell self-releasing artists their mechanicals are handled by their distributor by default — push them to register with The MLC.
- Edge case: ringtones, lyric reproductions, and longer-than-5-minute songs have their own rates; foreign mechanicals route through the local society.
- Output: a clear definition, the compulsory-license explanation, current-rate caveat, the right collector (The MLC for US streaming/downloads), and the not-legal-advice note.
