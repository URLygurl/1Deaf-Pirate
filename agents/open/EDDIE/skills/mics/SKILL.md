# Microphones

This skill gives EDDIE working knowledge of microphones: how they convert sound into electrical signal, the main transducer types and their character, polar patterns, and how to choose and place a mic for a given source. A microphone is a transducer that converts acoustic pressure variations into an electrical audio signal; the transducer type, diaphragm size, and polar pattern shape its sound and best use. Use it to recommend mics and placement for tracking. Canonical reference: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microphone

## Instructions

1. Know the main transducer types and their character: dynamic (moving-coil — rugged, handles high SPL, smooth, great for loud sources and live), condenser (capacitor — detailed, sensitive, wide frequency response, needs phantom power, ideal for studio vocals and acoustic detail), and ribbon (thin metal ribbon — warm, smooth top end, figure-8, often delicate and sensitive to high SPL/phantom power).
2. Factor diaphragm size: large-diaphragm condensers give a fuller, flattering, "bigger" sound (lead vocals, broad sources); small-diaphragm condensers ("pencils") give accurate, fast transient response (acoustic guitar, overheads, hi-hat).
3. Choose the polar pattern for the job: cardioid (front-focused, rejects rear — isolation, most common), omnidirectional (equal pickup all around — natural, flat, captures room, no proximity effect), figure-8/bidirectional (front and back, rejects sides — ribbons, mid-side, Blumlein), plus supercardioid/hypercardioid for tighter rejection.
4. Account for proximity effect: directional (cardioid/figure-8) mics boost low frequencies as the source gets closer — use it for warmth on close vocals, or back off / high-pass to avoid boom. Omnis don't exhibit it.
5. Provide phantom power (+48V) for condensers; never apply it carelessly to ribbons (many vintage ribbons can be damaged) — check the mic's spec.
6. Match mic to source: studio lead vocal → large-diaphragm condenser (or a dynamic like an SM7B/RE20 for control and rejection); loud guitar amp → dynamic (SM57) close on the cone, optionally blended with a ribbon; acoustic guitar → small-diaphragm condenser around the 12th fret; drum overheads → matched small-diaphragm condensers; kick → specialized dynamic inside, optional sub mic; snare → SM57; room → condenser or ribbon at a distance.
7. Place deliberately: distance and angle control tone and room amount; aim on/off-axis to brighten or darken; respect the 3:1 rule between multiple mics to minimize phase issues, and always check mics in mono/polarity when combining (e.g. top/bottom snare, close/room).
8. Manage gain and SPL: pad and high-pass where available for loud or close sources; pick a mic that handles the source's SPL without distorting.

## Examples

- User: "Dynamic or condenser for vocals?" → Explain condensers (esp. large-diaphragm) capture detail and air for most studio vocals; dynamics like the SM7B suit untreated rooms, loud/aggressive singers, and bleed rejection. Recommend based on room and voice.
- User: "How do I mic a guitar amp?" → Start with a dynamic (SM57) close on the speaker cone edge-to-center for tone, optionally blend a ribbon for warmth or a room mic for size; watch phase between mics.
- User: "What's a polar pattern?" → Define cardioid/omni/figure-8 and when to use each — cardioid for isolation, omni for natural/room capture, figure-8 for mid-side and rejecting the sides.
- User: "Why does my voice get boomy when I get close?" → Proximity effect on a directional mic boosts lows up close; back off slightly, high-pass, or use it intentionally for warmth (or switch to an omni to remove it).

## Guidelines

- Do match transducer type, diaphragm size, and polar pattern to the source, room, and SPL.
- Do mind phase/polarity and the 3:1 rule whenever combining multiple mics, and check in mono.
- Do use phantom power for condensers; verify before sending +48V to ribbons.
- Don't assume an expensive condenser beats a dynamic — the right tool depends on source and room.
- Don't ignore the room: an untreated space often favors dynamics or close cardioids over distant condensers.
- Edge case: ribbons are fragile and high-SPL/blast-sensitive; protect them and watch phantom-power compatibility.
- Output: recommend a mic type plus a concrete placement and pattern, and note proximity-effect and phase considerations.
