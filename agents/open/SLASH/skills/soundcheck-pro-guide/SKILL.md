# Soundcheck Like a Pro: Engineer's Best Practices

This skill equips SLASH with the higher-level craft and mindset that separates a professional soundcheck from an amateur one: preparation, gain discipline, listening strategy, communication, and avoiding the classic mistakes. Use it when the user wants to level up, not just learn the order. Canonical reference: https://www.gearnews.com/soundcheck-like-a-pro-an-engineers-guide/

## Instructions

1. Preach preparation: know the band, the input list, the room, and the gear before the band arrives. Pre-build a console template/snapshot so you're tuning, not patching, when the clock starts.
2. Insist on disciplined **gain staging**: set preamp gain with the source at true performance level, leave headroom for peaks, and don't chase level with the fader. Good gain makes everything downstream easier.
3. Listen to the **room, not just the channel**: account for how the empty room differs from a full house (a crowd absorbs high end and adds low-mid body), and don't over-tune to an empty room.
4. Use **subtractive EQ first** — find and notch the problem frequencies before boosting anything. High-pass everything that isn't bass to clean up low-end mud.
5. Solo and reference critically, but always re-check sources in the **context of the full mix**; instruments that sound great soloed can clash together.
6. Communicate clearly and economically with performers: give precise instructions ("play your loudest part," "hold a sustained chord"), keep energy up, and never leave the stage waiting.
7. Manage feedback proactively: set monitor and FOH gain-before-feedback margins, ring out where needed, and leave headroom so the show doesn't ring at peak moments.
8. Document the session: save snapshots, note showtime adjustments (rooms get brighter/quieter changes once full), and keep a running input/notes sheet.

## Examples

- User: "How do I soundcheck like a pro?" → Cover prep/templates, gain discipline, room-aware EQ, subtractive-first, full-mix context, tight communication, feedback margin, and documentation.
- User: "What are the most common soundcheck mistakes?" → Chasing level with faders instead of gain, over-EQing an empty room, soloing without checking the full mix, no high-pass discipline, poor communication leaving the band idle, no headroom for showtime.
- User: "Empty room sounds bright and harsh — what do I do?" → Don't over-correct; a full crowd absorbs highs and adds low-mid, so leave some top end and expect to trim lows at showtime.
- User: "How do pros keep soundcheck fast?" → Pre-built templates, clear instructions, work foundational-first, fix problems not preferences, save buffer time.

## Guidelines

- Frame everything around headroom, gain structure, and the empty-vs-full-room difference — these are the marks of a pro.
- Favor subtractive EQ and aggressive high-passing of non-bass channels.
- Stress communication and pace; a stalled stage is the most common amateur failure.
- Don't promise magic from plugins/processing — discipline beats gear.
