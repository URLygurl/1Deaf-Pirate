# Ringing Out: Eliminating Feedback to Raise Gain

This skill grounds SLASH in the concept and general technique of ringing out a sound system — deliberately driving the system toward feedback to identify the offending frequencies, then notching them with EQ so the system can run louder before it howls. It covers both FOH and monitors at the conceptual level. Canonical reference: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringing_out

## Instructions

1. Explain feedback first: it's the loop that forms when a microphone picks up sound from a loudspeaker, re-amplifies it, and the loudest resonant frequency in the room/system runs away into a sustained howl or ring.
2. Define **ringing out**: slowly raising system/monitor gain with a live mic open until feedback just begins, identifying the ringing frequency, and cutting it with a narrow EQ notch (graphic or parametric) to push the feedback point higher.
3. Give the procedure: open the relevant mic, slowly bring up gain until the system rings, identify the frequency by ear or with a graphic EQ/analyzer, apply a narrow cut at that frequency, then continue raising gain to find the next ring and repeat.
4. Note the goal is **gain-before-feedback margin** — a few notches usually buys meaningful extra level; the system is "rung out" when it has comfortable headroom before feedback at working level.
5. Identify common feedback regions: low-mid "boom" feedback around 100–250 Hz, midrange "honk" around 500 Hz–1 kHz, and piercing high feedback in the 2–6 kHz range. Use these as starting search areas.
6. Warn against over-notching: cutting too many frequencies or too deeply hollows out the tone. Stop once you have safe margin; a few well-placed narrow cuts beat a dozen deep ones.
7. Pair ringing out with good fundamentals: proper mic technique, high-passing channels, sensible mic/speaker placement, and not pushing gain past what the room allows.

## Examples

- User: "What does it mean to ring out a system?" → Explain raising gain to provoke feedback, finding the ringing frequency, and notching it to raise gain-before-feedback.
- User: "My vocal mic keeps feeding back — what do I do?" → Ring out: bring up gain to the ring, notch the offending frequency with a narrow cut, repeat for margin; also check mic placement, high-pass, and avoid pointing the mic at the speaker.
- User: "Where do feedback frequencies usually live?" → Low-mid ~100–250 Hz, mid honk ~500 Hz–1 kHz, and high pierce ~2–6 kHz as starting search ranges.
- User: "Can I just ring out forever for max volume?" → No — over-notching hollows the tone and there's a physical limit; aim for comfortable margin, not infinite gain.

## Guidelines

- Always use narrow notches (high Q / single graphic band) so you kill the ring without gutting the tone.
- Stop at safe margin; over-ringing-out is a real and common mistake.
- Frame everything around gain-before-feedback margin, not "removing feedback forever."
- Mention that mic technique, placement, and high-passing reduce how much ringing out you even need.
