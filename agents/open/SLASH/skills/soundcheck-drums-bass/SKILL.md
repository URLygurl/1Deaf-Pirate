# Soundchecking Drums & Bass: The Rhythm Foundation

This skill gives SLASH focused technique for checking the rhythm section — the kit and bass — which is the foundation every soundcheck builds on. It covers mic'ing, gain, EQ starting points, and getting kick and bass to lock together cleanly. Canonical reference: https://www.careersinmusic.com/sound-check-music/

## Instructions

1. Check the kit one piece at a time in foundational order: **kick → snare → toms → hi-hat → overheads → full kit together**. Have the drummer hit each at performance velocity, steadily.
2. **Kick:** set gain for a strong, clean transient with headroom; high-pass below the useful sub, then typically cut low-mid "box" (around 300–500 Hz) and boost click/beater (2–4 kHz) and weight (60–100 Hz) to taste. It must be felt and heard without booming.
3. **Snare:** gain for a healthy crack, high-pass to clean up, add body (~150–250 Hz) and crack/snap (~3–5 kHz) as needed; mind hi-hat bleed.
4. **Toms:** gate or manage bleed, add body in the low-mids and attack up top; check each tom individually then in fills.
5. **Hi-hat / overheads:** high-pass aggressively, capture sheen/cymbals up top; overheads define the overall kit picture, so balance them against the close mics.
6. **Bass:** take it via DI (and/or mic'd cab), set gain with headroom, high-pass only the sub rumble you don't need, then carve so it **shares low end with the kick without fighting it** — pick which owns the deep lows and which owns the punch (e.g., kick ~60–80 Hz, bass ~80–120 Hz, or vice versa), and add string/finger definition around 700 Hz–2 kHz.
7. **Lock kick + bass:** play them together and listen for masking and phase; if they're muddy, complementary-EQ them so each occupies its own range, and check timing/groove against the drummer.
8. Run the full kit, then kit + bass together as a groove before bringing in the rest of the band.

## Examples

- User: "How do I soundcheck the drums?" → Go piece by piece (kick → snare → toms → hat → overheads → full kit), gain-then-HPF-then-EQ each, then play the whole kit.
- User: "Kick and bass sound like mud together." → Decide who owns the deep lows vs the punch, complementary-EQ them into separate ranges, check phase/timing, and re-listen with both playing.
- User: "Give me a kick EQ starting point." → HPF the unused sub, cut box ~300–500 Hz, boost weight ~60–100 Hz and beater click ~2–4 kHz to taste — then trust your ears in the room.
- User: "Should I DI the bass or mic the amp?" → DI for clean, consistent control; mic the cab to capture amp character; many engineers blend both.

## Guidelines

- Always build the rhythm section first — the rest of the mix sits on it.
- Treat kick and bass as a pair: the fix for "muddy low end" is almost always frequency division between them, not just cutting one.
- EQ frequencies given are starting points, not rules — confirm by ear in the actual room.
- High-pass everything that isn't carrying real low end to clear up the kit.
