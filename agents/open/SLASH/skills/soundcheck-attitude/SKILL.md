# Effective Soundchecking: Attitude, Communication & Efficiency

This skill gives SLASH the human-factors craft of soundcheck — the attitude, communication style, and efficiency mindset that make a check go well regardless of gear. A good check is as much about managing people and time as it is about EQ. Canonical reference: https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/effective-soundchecking

## Instructions

1. Frame soundcheck as a shared goal, not a contest: the engineer and the band both want a great-sounding show. Set a calm, confident, collaborative tone.
2. Communicate with precise, plain instructions: tell each performer exactly what to play and for how long ("play your loudest section for me," "hold a sustained note"), and acknowledge when you've got what you need so they can stop.
3. Be decisive and efficient: keep the stage moving, avoid dead air, and don't disappear into headphones while the band stands idle. Idle time breeds frustration and tension.
4. Listen first, talk second, and don't over-tweak in front of an anxious band — small, confident moves read as competence; endless fiddling reads as panic.
5. Prioritize what matters to the performers: getting them a usable monitor mix early buys enormous goodwill and makes the rest of the check faster.
6. Handle difficult moments professionally: stay even-toned, don't argue about taste, solve the actual problem (often a monitor/feedback issue masquerading as attitude), and keep your stress off the talkback mic.
7. Close the loop: confirm everyone's happy, note showtime changes, thank the band, and leave them confident in you for the gig.

## Examples

- User: "How do I keep a band relaxed during soundcheck?" → Set a collaborative tone, give clear instructions, get monitors usable fast, keep things moving, and stay calm and decisive.
- User: "The lead singer is getting irritated at the check." → Likely a monitor/feedback problem; address it directly and calmly, give one clear fix at a time, don't take it personally, keep stress off the mic.
- User: "What's good soundcheck etiquette for engineers?" → Precise instructions, no dead air, acknowledge when you have what you need, prioritize monitors, stay even-toned, close the loop.
- User: "I freeze up and over-tweak when the band is watching." → Make small confident moves, listen before adjusting, trust your prep/templates, and remember idle fiddling reads as panic.

## Guidelines

- Lead with people and pace; the best technical check fails if the band loses confidence.
- Always give specific, bounded playing instructions and acknowledge completion.
- Get monitors usable early — it's the fastest path to a cooperative stage.
- Never broadcast stress or argue taste on an open mic.
- Defer the actual procedure to soundcheck-workflow and feedback fixes to ringing-out / ringing-monitors.
- Output should be practical, human, and confidence-building.
