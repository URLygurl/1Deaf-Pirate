# Monitor Engineer SOP: Dialing In the Stage

This skill gives SLASH a disciplined, repeatable standard operating procedure for the monitor engineer's side of soundcheck — getting every performer a stable, clear, feedback-free stage mix efficiently. Use it whenever someone is building or troubleshooting monitor mixes (wedges or IEMs). Canonical reference: https://soundgirls.org/dialing-it-in-part-two-strategies-and-a-suggested-standard-operating-procedure-for-soundcheck/

## Instructions

1. Prep before the band plays: confirm the monitor input list, how many mixes/sends you have, who gets what (wedge mixes by position, IEMs by person), and label every send clearly.
2. Verify the monitor rig itself first: check each wedge/IEM pack is powered, addressed to the right send, and producing clean signal at a safe level before anyone sings into it.
3. Ring out wedges proactively to establish gain-before-feedback margin on each open mic position before building mixes — defer the exact method to ringing-monitors, but bake it into the SOP.
4. Build mixes in a consistent order per performer: start with **their own primary source** (vocalist's own vocal, drummer's kick), then add the timing/pitch references they need (kick/bass for groove, a vocal or key for pitch), then taste items last.
5. Take requests in performer-centric language: "more me" first, then specific instruments. Make one change at a time and confirm it landed before moving on.
6. Keep stage volume under control — every extra dB in a wedge erodes feedback margin and bleeds into FOH mics. Encourage IEMs or modest wedge levels.
7. Walk the stage or use a talkback/SOLO routine to hear what each performer actually hears; don't mix monitors only from the console meters.
8. Snapshot/save each performer's mix and note showtime tweaks. Re-check at the full-band run because mixes change once everything is playing at once.

## Examples

- User: "Give me a monitor engineer SOP for a 5-piece." → Prep/label sends, verify rig, ring out wedges, build each mix (own source → references → taste), take "more me" passes one change at a time, full-band re-check, save.
- User: "Singer keeps asking for more of themselves but it's feeding back." → You're out of gain-before-feedback margin; ring out that wedge, high-pass the vocal, consider IEMs, and add a competing reference cut rather than only boosting the vocal.
- User: "How do I start a drummer's monitor mix?" → Start with kick (their anchor), add bass for groove, then a vocal/click reference, keep it loud enough to lock time but not blow up the stage.
- User: "Wedges vs in-ears for monitor world?" → IEMs give isolation, consistency, and far better feedback margin; wedges are simpler and need ringing out and tighter volume discipline.

## Guidelines

- Always establish feedback margin (ring out) before building wedge mixes, not after a howl on stage.
- Make one change per request and confirm it; chasing multiple changes blind wastes time and trust.
- Protect stage volume — it's the monitor engineer's primary lever over both feedback and FOH clarity.
- Speak in performer terms ("more you," "your kick") not console terms.
- Defer feedback technique to ringing-monitors and FOH order to soundcheck-workflow.
- Always save mixes and expect to re-balance at the full-band run.
