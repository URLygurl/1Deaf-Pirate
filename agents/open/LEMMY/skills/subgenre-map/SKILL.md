# Naked Subgenre Map

This skill is LEMMY's taxonomy of the heavy metal family tree. Apply it to classify bands, compare subgenres, and explain how each scene sounds, when it emerged, and who defined it. Canonical reference: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_genres

## Instructions

1. Identify whether the user wants a single definition, a comparison, a full map, or a band classification, and answer that shape directly.
2. Use this reference map of major subgenres (sound → era → defining bands):
   - **Traditional / heavy metal**: melodic riffs, soaring clean vocals, classic-rock roots; 70s–80s; Judas Priest, Iron Maiden, Dio, Saxon.
   - **NWOBHM**: faster, punk-energized British wave; late 70s–early 80s; Iron Maiden, Saxon, Diamond Head, Venom (see nwobhm).
   - **Speed metal**: faster, cleaner-than-thrash, melodic; early-mid 80s; Motörhead (proto), early Helloween, Agent Steel.
   - **Thrash metal**: fast palm-muted riffing, aggression, the "Big Four"; 80s; Metallica, Slayer, Megadeth, Anthrax (see thrash).
   - **Death metal**: growled vocals, blast beats, downtuned, brutal; mid-late 80s onward; Death, Morbid Angel, Cannibal Corpse, Obituary; technical/melodic (At the Gates, In Flames) offshoots.
   - **Black metal**: tremolo riffing, shrieked vocals, raw lo-fi production, atmosphere; first wave Venom/Bathory/Hellhammer (80s), second wave Norway 90s (Mayhem, Darkthrone, Emperor, Burzum).
   - **Doom metal**: slow, heavy, Sabbath-derived dread; 70s roots → 80s/90s; Candlemass, Saint Vitus, Pentagram, Electric Wizard; sludge (Eyehategod) and stoner (Sleep, Kyuss) adjacents.
   - **Power metal**: fast, melodic, anthemic, fantasy themes, double-kick; late 80s–90s; Helloween, Blind Guardian, DragonForce, Stratovarius (European), Manowar (US).
   - **Progressive metal**: complex song structures, odd meters, virtuosity; late 80s onward; Queensrÿche, Dream Theater, Tool, Opeth (prog-death).
   - **Glam / hair metal**: hooks, image, Sunset Strip; mid-80s; Mötley Crüe, Ratt, Poison, Twisted Sister.
   - **Groove metal**: mid-tempo, syncopated, post-thrash; early-mid 90s; Pantera, Sepultura, Machine Head, Lamb of God.
   - **Nu metal**: groove + hip-hop/alt influence, downtuned, often no solos; late 90s–2000s; Korn, Slipknot, Deftones, Limp Bizkit.
   - **Metalcore / deathcore**: metal + hardcore punk, breakdowns; 2000s; Killswitch Engage, As I Lay Dying; deathcore (Suicide Silence).
   - **Folk / symphonic / gothic metal**: fusion variants; 90s–2000s; Nightwish, Within Temptation (symphonic/gothic), Eluveitie, Finntroll (folk).
3. For comparisons, contrast on the axes that actually separate scenes: tempo, riff style, vocal technique (clean/growl/shriek/rap), production aesthetic, lyrical themes, geography, and era.
4. When classifying a band, name its primary subgenre, note any secondary/evolution (bands shift over careers), and flag genuinely contested cases.
5. Show lineage when useful: e.g., doom ← Black Sabbath; thrash ← NWOBHM + hardcore; death ← thrash extremity; black ← Venom/Bathory; groove ← thrash slowdown; nu ← groove + alt/hip-hop.

## Examples

- User: "Difference between death metal and black metal?" → Death: growled/guttural vocals, palm-muted downtuned riffs, blast beats, gory/visceral themes, often denser production (US/Florida & Sweden). Black: shrieked vocals, tremolo-picked riffs, raw/lo-fi atmosphere, occult/nature/anti-religious themes, Norwegian second wave defining the aesthetic.
- User: "Is Pantera thrash?" → Early Pantera flirted with glam, then defined **groove metal** with Cowboys from Hell (1990)/Vulgar Display of Power (1992) — thrash-derived but mid-tempo and syncopated, not thrash proper.
- User: "List the metal subgenres for me." → Give the map from Instruction 2, grouped roughly chronologically, 1–2 defining bands each.
- User: "What came out of thrash?" → Death metal (extremity), groove metal (slowdown), and influence on metalcore; sketch the lineage.

## Guidelines

- Do anchor each subgenre to sound + era + defining bands; that triad is the deliverable.
- Do acknowledge bands evolve and that subgenre lines are debated — give your call, then note the contest.
- Don't force a band into one box if it's a genuine hybrid; say "X with Y elements."
- Don't confuse adjacent scenes (speed vs thrash, groove vs nu, doom vs sludge vs stoner) — keep the distinctions sharp.
- For metal's overall origins/definition defer to metal-foundations; for NWOBHM, thrash, or Sabbath depth defer to their dedicated skills.
- Keep dates at decade/era granularity unless certain of a specific landmark year.
