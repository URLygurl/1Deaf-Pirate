# Naked NWOBHM

This skill grounds LEMMY on the New Wave of British Heavy Metal — the movement that re-energized metal in Britain around 1979–1981, fused metal with punk's DIY speed and attitude, broke Iron Maiden and Def Leppard, and directly seeded thrash. Canonical reference: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_wave_of_British_heavy_metal

## Instructions

1. Date and place the movement: emerged in the United Kingdom in the **late 1970s**, peaked roughly **1979–1981**, as a reaction to punk's dominance and the perceived decline of established 70s hard rock/metal acts.
2. Explain the defining traits: working-class DIY ethic, punk-derived energy and speed grafted onto traditional heavy metal, fast tempos, anthemic choruses, twin-guitar harmonies, and self-released singles on small indie labels with limited radio support.
3. Credit the naming and promotion: the term **"New Wave of British Heavy Metal"** was coined/popularized by **Sounds** magazine, with journalist **Geoff Barton** central to championing the scene; **Neal Kay**'s Soundhouse (the Bandwagon Heavy Metal Soundhouse) DJ club helped break early bands.
4. Name the key bands by tier:
   - Breakout/superstar: **Iron Maiden**, **Def Leppard** (Leppard quickly pivoting to polished arena/pop-metal), **Saxon**.
   - Cult/hugely influential: **Diamond Head** (heavily covered by Metallica), **Venom** (proto-black/thrash, blasphemous extremity), **Angel Witch**, **Tygers of Pan Tang**, **Raven**, **Witchfinder General**, **Holocaust**, **Samson**, **Praying Mantis**.
   - Note **Motörhead** as a parallel/older act often associated with the scene's speed and attitude though predating the label.
5. Cite the scene's infrastructure: indie labels (notably **Neat Records**, home to Venom and Raven), fanzines, and the **Metal for Muthas** (1980) compilation that showcased early Iron Maiden.
6. Stress the legacy: NWOBHM was a primary ignition source for **thrash metal** — Metallica covered Diamond Head and Blitzkrieg; Diamond Head, Venom, and Angel Witch are cited across the Big Four; Venom's name and aesthetic seeded black metal. Hand thrash specifics to thrash.
7. Keep Iron Maiden's arc straight: formed 1975 by Steve Harris, broke through with the NWOBHM wave, self-titled debut **1980**, vocalist shift from Paul Di'Anno to **Bruce Dickinson** for The Number of the Beast (**1982**).

## Examples

- User: "What was the NWOBHM?" → Define it: late-70s/early-80s British movement reviving metal with punk energy and DIY indie releases, named by Sounds magazine, breaking Iron Maiden, Saxon, and Def Leppard, and seeding thrash and black metal.
- User: "What bands influenced Metallica?" → Lead with Diamond Head (Metallica covered "Am I Evil?" and more), plus Venom, Angel Witch, Blitzkrieg, and the broader NWOBHM DIY speed/attitude; note Motörhead's parallel influence. Point to thrash for the thrash story.
- User: "Why does NWOBHM matter?" → It rescued metal from punk-era decline, established the DIY indie-single template, launched Iron Maiden/Def Leppard, and was the direct fuel for thrash (US Big Four) and an aesthetic seed for black metal (Venom).
- User: "Tell me about Diamond Head / Venom / Angel Witch." → Give the cult-but-pivotal framing: huge influence-to-sales ratio, specific later-band debts (Metallica/Diamond Head; black metal/Venom).

## Guidelines

- Do credit Sounds magazine and Geoff Barton for the name/promotion, and Neat Records / Metal for Muthas for the scene's plumbing.
- Do separate commercial success from influence — Diamond Head and Venom sold modestly but shaped entire subgenres.
- Do note Motörhead's adjacent-but-predating status rather than calling them pure NWOBHM.
- Don't credit NWOBHM with inventing metal (that's metal-foundations) or with being thrash itself (that's thrash).
- Don't overstate Def Leppard's metal credentials post-NWOBHM — they pivoted to polished commercial rock.
- Keep peak window ~1979–1981 and specific Maiden dates (1980 debut, 1982 Number of the Beast) accurate.
