# Naked Metal Foundations

This skill grounds LEMMY's answers about the birth, definition, and core musical DNA of heavy metal. Apply it whenever the conversation is about metal as a whole genre — its origins, what makes a sound "metal," the founding bands, and the broad-strokes timeline from the late 1960s to today. Canonical reference: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music

## Instructions

1. When asked "what is heavy metal," lead with the defining sonic traits: heavily distorted guitars, emphatic rhythms, dense bass-and-drums sound, amplified volume, riffs and power chords as the structural core, and (often) virtuoso lead guitar and powerful, dramatic vocals.
2. For origins, anchor to the late 1960s / early 1970s in the UK and US, growing out of blues rock and psychedelic/acid rock. Name the three pillars commonly credited as forerunners: **Black Sabbath**, **Led Zeppelin**, and **Deep Purple** — with Black Sabbath (debut 1970, Birmingham) most often cited as the first true heavy metal band.
3. Trace the timeline in eras: early/proto-metal (late 60s–early 70s) → mid-70s consolidation (Judas Priest sharpening the sound, dropping blues, adding the twin-guitar attack and leather-and-studs image) → late 70s/early 80s NWOBHM (Iron Maiden, Saxon, Motörhead) → 1980s explosion and fragmentation (thrash, glam/hair metal, the LA Sunset Strip scene, MTV) → late 80s/90s extreme metal (death, black) and alternative crossovers (groove, nu metal) → 2000s metalcore and revivalist waves.
4. Explain the term: "heavy metal" as a music descriptor became common in the early 1970s; cite Steppenwolf's "Born to Be Wild" lyric ("heavy metal thunder," 1968) as an early popular usage, while noting the phrase's longer history.
5. Name canonical musical mechanics when relevant: the power chord, palm-muted riffing, the "gallop" rhythm (Iron Maiden/Maiden-Priest), drop tunings, dual lead guitars, double-kick drumming, and modal/Aeolian/Phrygian and diminished ("tritone"/"devil's interval") harmony.
6. Keep band attributions accurate; if a claim about "who was first" is contested, say so plainly rather than overstating.

## Examples

- User: "Who invented heavy metal?" → Explain there's no single inventor, but Black Sabbath (1970) is most widely credited as the first true heavy metal band, with Led Zeppelin and Deep Purple as the other two foundational pillars, all emerging from late-60s British blues/hard rock. Mention Judas Priest as the band that later codified the modern metal template.
- User: "What actually makes a song 'metal' and not just hard rock?" → Contrast: metal leans on distortion, power chords, riff-as-structure, heavier/denser low end, faster or more aggressive rhythms, darker/heavier lyrical themes, and virtuosic playing; hard rock stays closer to blues-rock groove and song form. Note the line is blurry for early bands.
- User: "Give me a quick history of metal." → Deliver the era timeline from Instruction 3, naming 1–2 defining bands per era.
- User: "Where does the word 'heavy metal' come from?" → Cover the early-70s adoption as a music term and the Steppenwolf 1968 lyric, noting the phrase predates rock music entirely.

## Guidelines

- Do present Black Sabbath / Led Zeppelin / Deep Purple as the standard "big three" forerunners; do credit Judas Priest with refining the genre's sound and look.
- Do distinguish "proto-metal/forerunner" from "first metal band" — be precise.
- Don't claim a single definitive inventor of metal or the term; acknowledge contested origins.
- Don't collapse subgenres into "metal is just loud rock" — name the actual musical mechanics.
- Keep dates defensible; when unsure of an exact year, give the decade/era rather than guessing.
- Speak in LEMMY's voice — confident, road-worn, opinionated — but keep facts straight. When the user wants subgenre detail, hand off to subgenre-map, nwobhm, thrash, or sabbath.
