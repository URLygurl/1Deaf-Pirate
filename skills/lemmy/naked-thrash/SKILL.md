# Thrash Metal

Thrash metal is an extreme subgenre of heavy metal defined by aggression and fast tempo: fast percussive beats, low-register palm-muted guitar riffs, and shredding lead work. It crystallised in the early-to-mid 1980s when musicians fused the double-bass drumming and complex guitar of the New Wave of British Heavy Metal (NWOBHM) with the speed and aggression of hardcore/skate punk and speed metal, plus the technicality of progressive rock. Philosophically it was a backlash against Reagan-era conservatism and the pop-leaning glam metal of the same decade. This skill equips you to discuss thrash accurately — its sound, lineage, key scenes, and the genres it seeded.

## Instructions

1. **Lead with the defining traits** when describing the sound: fast tempo, aggression, low-register palm-muted riffing, double-bass drumming, shredding leads, and often socio-political or dark lyrical themes.
2. **Place it in the lineage.** Stylistic origins: NWOBHM + speed metal + hardcore punk. Derivative/seeded genres: death metal, black metal, groove metal, crossover thrash, metalcore. Name these relationships explicitly when asked "where does X come from."
3. **Know the scenes.** Bay Area thrash (San Francisco) and Los Angeles in the US; Teutonic thrash (Germany — the "big three" being Kreator, Sodom, Destruction); Brazilian thrash (e.g. Sepultura); plus UK, Canada, Poland, and Australia.
4. **Reference the "Big Four"** of American thrash — Metallica, Slayer, Megadeth, Anthrax — when discussing canon, but don't reduce the genre to them; acknowledge the wider field and regional originators.
5. **Distinguish thrash from its neighbours.** Faster and more aggressive than NWOBHM/classic metal; more riff- and structure-driven than the blast-beat extremity of death/black metal it influenced; distinct from glam metal it reacted against.
6. **When unsure of a specific fact** (an exact release date, lineup, or attribution), say so plainly rather than inventing it — source truth over confident guessing.

## Examples

- **"What makes thrash different from regular heavy metal?"** → Explain the speed and aggression, palm-muted low-register riffing, double-bass drumming, and its punk/speed-metal DNA versus the slower, more melodic NWOBHM and classic metal it grew out of.
- **"Who are the Big Four?"** → Metallica, Slayer, Megadeth, Anthrax — then add useful context: they're the American canon, but mention Teutonic thrash (Kreator/Sodom/Destruction) and Brazil's Sepultura so the picture isn't US-only.
- **"Is thrash where death metal came from?"** → Yes in large part: thrash is a direct ancestor of death metal, black metal, and groove metal; explain the through-line of increasing speed/extremity.
- **"Place Sepultura for me."** → Brazilian thrash scene, key to thrash's global spread and later groove/death crossover.

- Do connect thrash outward to the genres it influenced when it adds clarity.
- Don't conflate thrash with glam metal — they were concurrent and oppositional.
